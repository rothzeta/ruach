# Suggestions from the RAMEC orchestration (Claude Coordinator, 2026-10-05 to 2026-10-08)

Source: one Claude Code Coordinator session (Opus) orchestrating RAMEC, a band-music generator (.NET server and Band engine, Vue client), with Sol (Codex `gpt-6.1-sol`) implementers in Herdr worktrees, Sonnet merge stewards, Opus council chairs and designers, and Gemini (through `agy`) council members. The owner asked for this document on 2026-10-08: "create a suggestions from claude orchestration in ruach docs, the skills, agents, workflows adn general recomendations from your feedback".

Everything below is a **proposal** for Ruach. Names and packaging follow the roadmap convention: proposals until decided. Where a suggestion matches a planned 0.4 item, it says so.

## 1. What the run looked like

- **Scale on 2026-10-08 alone:** 20 units merged one at a time (status items 32–50 in RAMEC `docs/plans/2026-10-05-6df23d00-player-v0-status.md`), 3 councils (a small one, a UX review and a seven-member grand council), a design-tool trial, a design prototype and an ASD-STE100 code explanation.
- **Shape of one unit:** the Coordinator writes a self-contained brief → creates a Herdr worktree → `just install` → starts Sol with `--approve-for-me` → sends the brief → arms a background wait → reads the hand-back → launches a merge-steward agent → the steward waits for the previous merge, merges, runs `just check ORIG_HEAD`, writes the status item and cleans up.
- **Shape of one council:** brief with binding owner decisions quoted → chair writes evidence → independent round 1 (members cannot read each other) → chair's draft → up to three APPROVE/CHANGES rounds → result with owner decisions and recommendations.

## 3. Skills

### 3.1 `ruach-workflow-council` (planned 0.4; specimen from this run)

RAMEC's council briefs are a working specimen (`docs/reviews/2026-10-08/grand-council-kernel/brief.md`, `council-player-timeline/brief.md`). Proposed body:

- **Brief contents:** the owner's words verbatim; binding owner decisions quoted, "do not change"; required inputs; numbered questions; members with fields and harness; the process; the output folder; "read-only except this folder".
- **Round 0, evidence:** the chair verifies the inputs against the code (file:line) and writes `evidence.md` before anyone proposes.
- **Round 1 independence:** members write `r1-<n>-<role>.md` without reading other r1 files. Enforce by sending each member only its own prompt and checking file read order where the harness allows it.
- **Synthesis and review:** the chair's `draft.md` credits every idea and marks disagreements; members answer APPROVE or CHANGES with concrete replacement text; at most three rounds; unresolved points go to the owner, not to a vote.
- **Result:** `result.md` with a word budget, the approval record per round, open disagreements, and owner decisions each with a recommendation and the members who hold each side. `sources.md` for external sources.
- **Mixed models:** Sol members through Herdr (one worktree each, removed at the end), Gemini members through `agy` with prompt files, Claude members as subagents. Record any substitution in the result.
- **Save as you go:** every file is written to the folder as soon as it exists; the Coordinator commits intermediate rounds (RAMEC did at round 2) and the result on hand-back.
- **Explaining both sides:** when the owner asks "what are the arguments on both sides", the Coordinator answers from the r-files with attribution. Keep the per-member position in the result so this is possible without re-reading every round.

### 3.2 `ruach-decision-register` (new skill)

The single most valuable addition from this run (F1, F3).

- **One register per project** (RAMEC: `docs/reviews/2026-10-08/roadmap/decisions.md`, section "Owner rulings").
- **Every owner message that decides, approves, rejects or states intent** is appended verbatim with its date and what it answers, in the same turn, then committed. Rulings derived from it are listed below the quote with stable IDs (DEC-n, K-n, PD-n).
- **Authority labels:** owner ruling, orchestrator decision (marked as such and listed for owner review), member proposal. Only owner rulings are architecture.
- **Open items carry forward** with their ID until ruled; a reply that does not name IDs is mapped explicitly ("I read '1 ok' as PD-1 …") and confirmed when two open lists could match.
- **Absence is a finding:** when the owner cites a ruling that is not in the register, say so plainly with where you searched; never reconstruct it from memory.
- **Supersession:** a later ruling that overturns an earlier one names it (RAMEC KERNEL-1 overturned the v0 plan's exclusion of a generic `PlayerKernel`).

### 3.3 `ruach-work-ledger` (new skill, or a Coordinator section)

- A committed "In flight and queued" section in the project's status file: running units (brief path, branch, worker), queued units in order with their dependency reason, items waiting for the owner.
- Updated on every start, merge and queue change, in the same commit as the status item where possible.
- Sequencing the Coordinator chose is marked "orchestrator".
- The ledger survives context compaction; after a compaction the Coordinator re-reads it before acting.

### 3.4 Merge queue: extend `ruach-workflow-feature` §5 or add `ruach-workflow-merge-queue`

The feature workflow integrates one feature. RAMEC needed a queue of many parallel units into one master:

- One steward per hand-back; a steward **waits until the previous branch is deleted and its status commit is on master** before merging. Several stewards can be launched at once and serialize themselves.
- The steward reviews the hand-back against the brief: ownership, regression-first evidence, no loosened assertions, budgets met or explicitly moved; orchestrator dispositions are passed in the steward's prompt, labelled as such.
- One `just check ORIG_HEAD` after the merge, in the foreground, logged to a known path; rerun once only for a registered flake (§3.9).
- The steward writes the next status item, cleans up (worktree, Herdr workspace, branch) and appends a ledger line; it never pushes.
- Stewards were Sonnet; review quality was adequate and cheap. They flagged useful smells (duplicated projection logic, bare literals) that fed later briefs.

### 3.5 Herdr worker operation: extend `ruach-herdr` beyond launch

`ruach-herdr` stops at launch. Most Herdr friction was after launch (F4–F6):

- **Send and verify:** send text, send Enter, poll the pane until it shows the harness's working marker, and resend Enter once if the text is still in the input. Provide this as one command.
- **Pending questions:** Codex scope questions appear as a selectable prompt (`shift+←`, choose, Enter). The Coordinator answers them; conditions go in a follow-up message.
- **Startup readiness:** after `agent start`, confirm the harness is interactive (not a shell after a self-update) before sending the assignment.
- **Escalation allowlist:** never approve escalations on the worker's behalf. Each assignment names the commands the worker may request escalation for (here: `just check`, `just test-project`, git staging, `just commit`, a dev server for measurement). "No escalation" without exceptions blocks commits in linked worktrees.
- **Pane registry:** keep `unit → pane` in a file (RAMEC `.orchestrator/panes.txt`) so stewards can close the right workspace.
- **Worktree base:** always `--cwd <repo>`; a missing `--cwd` once created a worktree in another repository.

### 3.6 Hand-back waits (new, small)

- The Coordinator never ends a turn with workers running and no background wait armed.
- A wait fires when any named unit's hand-back reaches a terminal or decision state **that the Coordinator has not yet handled**, independent of when the wait started (F7). Keep the handled state in a small file.
- Waits for external systems (CI) poll at the system's natural cadence.

### 3.7 Assignment (brief) authoring: the 0.4 assignment templates

- **One complete outcome**, the owner's words and the rulings it implements quoted with their register IDs, explicit owned files, focused verification then one full check, the shared rules footer (regression first, values not winners, mechanical edits allowed and listed, stop on foreign files, commit command, hand-back file).
- **Checklist before sending (F8):** the tests beside each owned source file; test-support hosts; every literal a version bump touches; docs rows the change makes stale; files another running unit owns (name them and the unit).
- **UI briefs:** a frame budget with real-size data and named viewports (§3.8).
- **Briefs are files** in a known folder, so stewards and later briefs can cite them.

### 3.8 `ruach-ui-budgets` (new skill)

- Every UI unit states a per-interaction frame budget (16 ms) measured with **real-size data** (RAMEC: the largest real song, 58 decisions of up to 748 candidates, 0.8 MB per decision) at **named viewports** (3440 and 1920 CSS px, plus phone width).
- Say which environment measured it: a component host (script only) is not a browser (layout and paint). Both are reported; acceptance names one.
- Report before and after; a budget that cannot be met in the unit is moved to a named later unit with the measured numbers as its input, never silently dropped.
- Wide-screen rule from the RAMEC UX review: work areas full-bleed, forms and prose at a readable measure; no page cap.

### 3.9 Flake register (extend `ruach-testing`)

- A register of known environment flakes with the symptom, where it occurs and the fixed disposition ("rerun once; never edit the test"), cited by every brief and steward prompt.
- A flake that recurs in the main environment becomes a regression-first unit, not a register entry.

### 3.10 `ruach-ste-writing` (new, optional)

The owner asked for an explanation "in asd-ste100"; the result was useful and reusable. A small skill: ASD-STE100 rules (short sentences, approved words, one instruction per sentence, active voice), a file:line reference for every claim, "describe as it is, recommend nothing", and a docs-versus-code drift section. It could also be a Librarian mode.

## 4. Agents and roles

- **Merge steward** (new role or an Implementer mode): reviews and merges one hand-back by a procedure file and reports in a few lines. Bash and Read only. Sonnet was sufficient.
- **Council chair** (Architect variant or a council-workflow role): writes evidence and the result, drives members, credits ideas, records the approval per round. Opus.
- **Council member**: consumer-declared domain roles (musician, researcher, utility-AI, instrument specialist, critic) through existing routing, as the roadmap already says. Members get a read-only assignment that writes one file.
- **Designer** (consumer-declared): produces a design document plus a self-contained prototype on real data, bound to recorded fields and the existing palette, and lists "not recorded" separately (F10).
- **Coordinator additions** (`agents/coordinator.md`): keep the decision register (§3.2) and the ledger (§3.3); commit its own documents immediately (F12); re-read both after a context compaction; when the owner's statement conflicts with the record, report the conflict before acting.

## 6. General recommendations

1. **Repository over memory.** Anything the owner says that matters, any council output and any plan the Coordinator follows is committed the turn it exists. Chat is a view, not storage.
2. **Owner words verbatim, rulings by ID.** It made later questions ("what did I approve?") answerable in one lookup.
3. **One unit, one outcome, one merge.** Small units merged one at a time kept `just check ORIG_HEAD` meaningful and conflicts rare; 20 merges in one day with no revert.
4. **Regression first, values not winners.** Briefs required a failing public test before the fix and forbade asserting musical winners, object identity or reflection; stewards checked it. It held across all units.
5. **Real-size data in every budget.** Toy fixtures hid every UI cost found today (974 ms zoom, 518 ms first mount, 73.5 ms resize).
6. **Name the authority of every decision.** "Orchestrator decision, not owner architecture" in briefs and status items kept the owner's control visible and reversible.
7. **Prefer moving a budget over relaxing it.** When a unit could not meet a budget without foreign files, the budget moved to the unit that owns those files, with the numbers as input.
8. **Measure the Coordinator.** Re-measure polling and idle time with the session-usage script (roadmap); this run's idle panes (F4) and dead wait (F7) would show up there.
9. **Keep stewards and members cheap, the Coordinator lean.** The Coordinator read hand-backs, diffs summaries and results, and delegated source reading; its context still filled once per few hours. The ledger (§3.3) is what makes compaction safe.
